node:test unit suites run with `npx tsx --test` from cwd=server/. The cross-instance cases are driven by constructing two independent store instances bound to one data file in a temp directory — separate in-memory state and separate mutation chains over one filesystem, which is what distinguishes them from REL-BGSTAB-020's single-instance fixture. AC-2 and AC-3 are driven deterministically by interposing a peer write on the temp path between a publish's write and its rename, rather than by racing and sampling: a statistical fixture cannot distinguish a publish that moved a peer's bytes from a peer that published afterwards. AC-4, AC-5 and AC-6 are driven by injecting rename failures with specific errno codes at the filesystem boundary, because the transient codes concerned are Windows semantics and are not reproducible natively on the Linux/WSL2 host used; the retry budget is asserted by counting attempts and elapsed waits, not by wall-clock tolerance. Boundary controls assert that a non-transient failure rejects on the first attempt without consuming the budget, that an exhausted budget leaves the pre-existing destination intact and parseable, and that a publish's failure cleanup removes only its own temp file. The characterisation scripts that produced the measurements in the statement are preserved under docs/analysis/kiwi-srs-2026-09-16.buildergate.atomic-store-write/raw/ and are characterisation evidence, not part of the regression suite.
