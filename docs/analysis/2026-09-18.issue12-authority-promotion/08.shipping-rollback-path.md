## What the shipping path does with the epoch

`rollbackSession` (`TerminalAuthorityProductionAdapter.ts:4382`) computes

```ts
const nextStreamEpoch = nextOrdinal(state.streamEpoch);   // nextOrdinal = BigInt(v) + 1n
```

and passes it to `manager.beginTerminalAuthorityRollback(...)`, which delegates straight to
`controller.beginRollback(request)` **without touching `retained.committedFactKeys`**.

Instrumented at the `SessionManager` entry and run against three suites: **80 probe hits (40
entries, 40 exits)**, all from `TerminalAuthorityController.test.ts`. The requested epoch is
**strictly greater than the current retained epoch in 40 of 40**:

| retained epoch at entry | requested `nextStreamEpoch` | count |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 9 | 28 |
| 7 | 10 | 7 |
| 8 | 10 | 2 |
| 7 | 11 | 2 |
| 7 | 12 | 1 |

**So `06`'s Result 3 — the epoch moving backwards, 2 → 1 — is confined to the
debug-isolation restore and is not a property of the shipping path.** That resolves an open
item in production's favour, and it means `MIG-BGSTAB-002` AC-5's *`새 streamEpoch`* is
satisfied by the path that ships.

**Held to what the probe measured, which is less than it may appear.** The exit rows show
`retainedEpochAfter=7` on **28 successful** rollbacks whose request named epoch 9 — so
`beginRollback` returning `ok: true` **starts a transaction rather than moving the epoch**, and
my probe reads before the commit. The measured claim is therefore about the **request**: the
shipping path never asks for a lower or equal epoch. Whether the retained epoch ends higher
after commit is **not** established here.

## What the shipping path does with the dedup ledger

`beginTerminalAuthorityRollback` does not clear, restore or otherwise touch
`committedFactKeys`. It simply persists on `retained`, carrying keys prefixed with the **old**
epoch.

Combined with the key shape measured in `05` and `06` — `${streamEpoch}:${sourceSeq}:${ordinal}`
— this is the decisive statement for issue AC-11:

> **On the shipping path the retention clause is vacuous.** The epoch always advances, the
> epoch is the key's first component, so no retained key can ever match a fact derived after
> the rollback. "Retain the dedup ledger until the epoch stabilises" cannot prevent a
> re-settlement, because nothing it holds is reachable any more.

That is not a defect in the code. It is a criterion asking a mechanism to do something its key
design does not permit, and it is the clarification to bring to `/kiwi-srs`: **the criterion
must say what the retention is for.** If the intent is cross-epoch dedup, the key needs a
component that survives the epoch — which is where issue AC-4's absent `factType` and
`valueHash` become relevant, and the two gaps turn out to be one.

## And the corpus cannot pose the question on this path either

**`ledgerKeys=0` and `facts=0` in 40 of 40** shipping-rollback entries — the same disjointness
`06` measured on the debug path, now confirmed on the path that ships. Across **both** paths,
**64 rollback events, zero with a populated ledger.**

The control is unchanged and still fires: `RetainedTerminalAuthority.test.ts` produces **196**
fact-key additions reaching a ledger of **size 81**, so the ledger demonstrably can be
non-empty. Both zeroes are measurements.

## A third correction, and it is the same direction again

`05` raised the possibility that retained keys from superseded epochs accumulate as dead
weight. Measured: `removeRetainedTerminalFacts` (`SessionManager.ts:8241`) **evicts the key
alongside its fact**, reconstructing the identical key string and decrementing
`ledgerFactKeyEncodedBytes`. The ledger is bounded by the fact-retention policy. **There is no
unbounded-growth defect.**

That is the **third** correction in this lane moving the same way — toward production being
more correct than my search-derived read suggested (the `try`-less enqueue, the rollback
counter, now ledger growth). At three, it is a property of my method rather than three
accidents, and it should be weighted as such: **my reads of this codebase are biased
pessimistic, and my executed measurements have not moved in either direction.** Anything below
that I have *read* rather than *run* should be discounted accordingly — including the vacuity
claim above, which rests on reading that `beginRollback` does not touch the ledger, plus the
measured fact that the requested epoch always advances.
