## What was added, and why these two

§5 and §6 of this bundle measured two properties of issue #11 as **covered but unowned** —
the disposition that looks identical to "covered" in any coverage measurement and behaves
like "uncovered" under maintenance. Each now has a test whose *name is the property*.

Before inventing predicates, the repository was searched for ones it already trusts. It has
one: `ACK-01`'s *"routes each reservation tuple **unchanged** after commit"*. That is the
right predicate for issue AC-9, and it had simply never been pointed at renderer delivery.

### Guard 1 — `REL-BGSTAB-011 AC-4/AC-9 — the shadow changes neither the delivered bytes nor the authority decision`

Two arms over identical input, one with `retainedShadowEnabled: true` and one `false`,
asserting delivered-chunk **equality** across arms, delivered payload equality against the
emitted bytes, and equality of the `authorityRevision` and `screenSeq` sequences.

It asserts its **preconditions before its property**: that the two arms really are in
`shadow` and `disabled` mode, and that each delivered **exactly one** chunk. Without those
the test would pass over an empty delivery list — a universal claim over an empty set is
true — or over two arms that had silently stopped being different experiments.

Equality rather than `includes` is the whole point. The existing AC-9 assertion
`deliveries.some(entry => entry.data.includes(…))` is true of the correct payload and of one
whose tail the shadow has eaten.

### Guard 2 — `REL-BGSTAB-011 AC-3 — an over-cap retained checkpoint converges without a silent tail`

Emits 24 lines under `maxSnapshotBytes: 16`, then asserts
`checkpoint.serializedData === checkpoint.rehydrateAnsi`, the exact byte length, the exact
retained logical-line count (24), and the survival of both the newest and the oldest
retained line.

It also asserts its **precondition**: that the payload genuinely exceeds the legacy cap
(998 bytes against a cap of 16). Asserting the property on an under-cap payload would pass
for the wrong reason, which is the scenario-error shape rather than the predicate-error one.

Every fixture number here was **read off a real run** before being pinned, not recalled:
998 bytes, 24 logical lines, one delivered chunk per arm, `authorityRevision` `[1]`.

## What these guards still do not cover

Guard 1 addresses **Route B** of §5 only — the shadow's *presence* altering delivered bytes.
**Route A** — the comparer's *verdict* changing delivery — remains **unfalsifiable through
the public surface**, because the verdict is `unavailable` at all 289 delivery decisions the
suite makes. A guard cannot close that; only a fixture that makes the verdict available at a
delivery decision could, and no such state is reachable through the public surface today.
Recorded as the third disposition, **not** counted as coverage.

Neither guard covers delivery **ordering**, delivery **timing**, or shadow-driven mutation of
`authorityEpoch`. Two routes and three loss modes were injected across this lane; that is
not all routes and not all modes.
