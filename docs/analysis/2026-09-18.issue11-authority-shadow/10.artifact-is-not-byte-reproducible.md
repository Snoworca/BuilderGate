# Issue #11 — the parity artifact is not byte-reproducible, so a whole-file hash pin on it cannot hold

Found while verifying the tree was clean after committing §8's guard fix: the artifact came
back **modified** after a single re-run of its own producer.

## The measurement

`node tools/wave3/retained-shadow-parity.test.mjs` run three times against an unchanged
tree:

| run | artifact sha256 |
|---|---|
| committed | `b68df22c4400363362a80494c95bba8348522aee38dc1431e95b76185002b772` |
| re-run 1 | `aee9aeb945176c542cabe1ca2a1303b6cc396baca0d4b469eaed39efd48a247e` |
| re-run 2 | `09f7935e4be76c0adde7a7c5ad2cfd7815d6be3e2fd01bdcf7097b723bffa47c` |

Three runs, three hashes.

## Which fields move, and the correction to my own first statement

A structural diff between two runs reports **four** differing fields. My first reading was
"the artifact is not reproducible", and that overstates the mechanism — **two of the four
are not non-determinism at all**:

| field | cause |
|---|---|
| `capturedAt` | wall-clock timestamp — **genuinely non-deterministic** |
| `focusedRun.stdoutSha256` | hashes the runner's stdout, which carries per-test durations — **genuinely non-deterministic** |
| `inputHashes.files["docs/spec/30.buildergate-stability.srs.md"]` | this lane added VE-9 to the SRS *between* the two runs — **a real input change** |
| `inputHashes.sourceSetSha256` | derived from the row above — **a real input change** |

So the accurate statement is: **two fields are non-deterministic by construction.** That is
sufficient to make a whole-file sha256 pin unsatisfiable, but it is a narrower claim than
the one I first wrote, and the narrower one is what the measurement supports.

## Why this matters for the downstream pin

§7 recorded that `tools/wave3/authority-promotion-evidence.test.mjs:31` hard-pins this
artifact's whole-file sha256, and recommended a one-line update as `#12`'s call.

**That recommendation was too small, and this measurement is why.** A whole-file hash pin on
this artifact **can never be satisfied by re-running the producer** — only by never running
it. It held historically because the artifact was committed once, in another tree, and never
regenerated. That is the same property that let it drift out of agreement with this tree
while still matching its own seal: §1 row 6 matched, and §7 measured that its recorded
production hashes were 4 of 4 wrong.

**A seal that is only satisfiable by not re-running the thing it seals is measuring "nobody
has re-run this", not "this is still true."** That is the disconnected-instrument class: it
runs, it passes, and it checks something other than its name.

So the disposition is not "update the pin". It is: **pin the fields that carry the claim**
— `verdict`, the focused totals, `pinnedTestNamesMissing`, `expectedTestNamesSha256`, the
gate's `reason` — rather than the whole file. Those are stable across runs and they are what
`verifyRedProductionUnchanged` actually wants to know.

This lane did **not** make that change. It is `#12`'s guard, the recommendation has now
moved from one line to a small redesign, and both are the orchestrator's call. The artifact
in the tree is left at the committed `b68df22c…`.

## Standing caveat this reinforces

The PH-004 reports' sealed manifest (§1) includes this artifact. Its hash there was never
evidence that the numbers around it were true — only that the bytes had not changed since
someone wrote them. A checksum proves an artifact has not changed since sealing; it says
nothing about whether it was correct when sealed, and here it could not even have been
re-derived to check.
