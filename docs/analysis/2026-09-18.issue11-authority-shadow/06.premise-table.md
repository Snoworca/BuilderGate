## Row 9 in full — the one real gap

Two routes by which a shadow could influence delivery were injected at
`SessionManager.ts:8012`:

| Route | Result | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| the comparer's **verdict** gates delivery (M3) | site reached **289 times**, `comparer.result` `unavailable` on **289/289**, mutation **never fired** | **dead mutation → the property is *unfalsifiable through the public surface*** |
| the shadow's **presence** alters delivered bytes by one (M4) | fired **105 times**, caught by **4** tests | **covered but unowned** — all four are named for `PERF-BGSTAB-011` / `ACK-16` properties |
| — as judged by the test named for AC-9 | `REL-BGSTAB-011 AC-7` **passed** under M4 | **does not discriminate** |

The last row is the Class B shape: `deliveries.some(e => e.data.includes(substring))` is true
of the correct payload and of a payload the shadow has corrupted at its tail. It is a
presence check carrying an integrity check's name.

Route A's dead mutation is not a criticism of the tests — it is a fact about the system.
The comparer samples only at global headless idle under a ≥5 s low-duty interval while
delivery is decided synchronously, so in the committed suite the verdict is never available
where delivery is decided. **Not covered, not uncovered: the third disposition.**

**Bound: two routes are not all routes.** Delivery ordering, delivery timing and mutation of
`authorityEpoch`/`authorityRevision` by the shadow were not injected, and nothing here says
they are guarded.

to be violated — the instrument is broken, not the product — and I did not measure the
invariant independently of this fixture. So the finding is *the checkbox has no passing
named verification on this platform*, not *the behaviour is wrong*. I am **not** unchecking
it unilaterally; it is raised for the orchestrator.
