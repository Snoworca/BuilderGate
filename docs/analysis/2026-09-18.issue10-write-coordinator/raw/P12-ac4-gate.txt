## A dead mutation, and how it was distinguished from a weak test
The FIRST stall injection targeted the two pump() calls at lines 990 and 1044. It
SURVIVED — and that looked exactly like a weak guard. It was not: those two sites do not
govern the checkpoint slice loop. The tell was that the observable event log was BYTE
IDENTICAL to the un-mutated run, which a real stall cannot be. The actual resume point is
the pump() at ~line 1031, inside the lifecycleCanContinue block of the checkpoint slice
completion handler. Re-injected there, the flow changes and the guard reddens.

A surviving mutation has two causes — the test is weak, or the mutation never reached the
path. They are distinguished by checking that the mutation changed the observable behaviour
at all before concluding anything about the test. A dead mutation is a dead control.

Production restored byte-identically after every injection (sha256 verified each time).
